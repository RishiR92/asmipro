# Hoys — intelligence with craft

## Brand story
Hoys is the intelligence behind people who do real work. It brings paid jobs and takes care of the office work, while the pro stays in control. The logo should communicate **capable, established, human** before it communicates any particular trade.

The ambition is a category-defining AI company—not a local handyman business. Express that through exceptional lettering, restraint, and one memorable detail, not futuristic effects or a literal toolbox.

## What changes from the rejected logos
- Only **hoys**: no separate symbol, no domain or tagline in the main logo.
- Four genuinely different lettering ideas, not font or color swaps of the previous designs.
- Explore a trade detail as part of a letter or attached directly to it; never place a tool beside the name.
- Keep a clean, tool-free direction as the benchmark. A motif earns its place only if it looks better and remains readable.

## Reference principles
Instinct.com currently uses a white homepage, dark onyx text, and Aime typography, with restrained visual detail. Its broader styles include Melange, muted sand, red, and teal. Borrow the confidence and human tone—not its lettering, mascot, or layout.

## Four directions to develop
1. **Quiet authority — recommended baseline.** A finely balanced lowercase design with moderate stroke weight, open counters, and subtle flared terminals. Confident and human rather than heavy and bubbly. Distinctive through the relationship between the h, o, y, and s alone.
2. **Precision fit.** Architectural sans-serif lettering with a restrained fitting or joint suggested where the h shoulder meets its stem. A crafted construction detail, not a recognizable plumbing-only pictogram.
3. **The hanging detail.** Refined lettering with a tiny simplified wrench hooked directly onto the y terminal. The name remains dominant; the attachment is a discovery at larger sizes. Also show a clean small-size version without the attachment.
4. **Crafted intelligence.** A contemporary low-contrast serif treatment with robust proportions and one quietly tooled terminal. Editorial elegance without fragile hairlines, vintage tradesman styling, or luxury-fashion imitation.

## Color exploration
Treat these as proposed comparison palettes, not a decision to recolor the website.
- **Ink + warm white:** near-black `#202321`, warm white `#FAF8F3`. Strongest starting point for timelessness and broad use.
- **Cobalt + warm white:** cobalt `#1E3FBF`, warm white `#FAF8F3`. Connects to the current site, with a single-color logo rather than multicolored letters.
- **Deep green + white:** mineral green `#244A40`, white `#FFFFFF`. A quieter alternative expressing grounded confidence; compare against cobalt rather than assume it is better.
- Avoid gradients, chrome, glow, primary lime lettering, AI sparkles, and tool-colored decorations. Every concept must work in one color first.

## Deliverables after approval
- Four initial logo concepts as PNGs, shown at equal optical size on a clean comparison sheet.
- For each concept: a transparent PNG plus a light-background preview; inspect dark placements and provide a reversed version where required.
- Recommend one direction based on distinctiveness, readability, fit with the current site, and flexibility beyond home services.
- Refine the chosen candidate from its actual image, then deliver tightly framed transparent light/dark PNGs with useful margins.
- No website replacement or site-wide rename until you choose and authorize the logo.

## Production and checks
Use the logo-design workflow and premium image generation for bespoke lettering. Render the exact name **hoys** with no extra words. Inspect every result at full size and at approximately 80–120 px wide / 24–32 px high; compare a 30 px-high placement against the current header. Reject malformed letters, pale removal fringes, inconsistent spacing, distracting tools, and fragile small details. PNG is the requested deliverable, not an editable vector; vector redraw can be a separate later step.
