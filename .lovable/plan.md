# Hoys — intelligence with craft

## Brand story
Hoys is the intelligence behind people who do real work. It brings paid jobs and takes care of the office work, while the pro stays in control. The logo should communicate **capable, established, human** before it communicates any particular trade.

The ambition is a category-defining AI company—not a local handyman business. Express that through exceptional lettering, restraint, and one memorable detail, not futuristic effects or a literal toolbox.

## What changes from the rejected logos
- Only **hoys**: no separate symbol, no domain or tagline in the main logo.
- Four genuinely different lettering ideas, not font or color swaps of the previous designs.
- Build the home-services reference into the structure of a letter, not a separate icon or a tool hanging like a charm.
- All four proposed concepts include one integrated detail. Use ordinary, tool-free lettering only as an internal readability benchmark.

## Reference principles
Instinct.com currently uses a white homepage, dark onyx text, and Aime typography, with restrained visual detail. Its broader styles include Melange, muted sand, red, and teal. Borrow the confidence and human tone—not its lettering, mascot, or layout.

OpenAI's official brand guidance emphasizes custom lettering that balances geometric precision with an approachable character. Sierra supplies monochrome and reversed logo variants. The useful shared principle is disciplined typography and adaptable reproduction—not a color that magically signals a valuation. No palette guarantees trust or company scale.

Small-size reproduction is a deciding constraint: SanDisk's identity case study describes dropping a symbol partly because it did not print well on very small products. For Hoys, a tool reference should be one simple structural gesture rather than a detailed illustration. Color and motif recommendations below are design judgments, not measured conversion claims.

## Four directions to develop
### Best natural fit: the y as an open-ended wrench
The **y already has two arms and a long descending stroke**: the arms can suggest the wrench's open jaws, while the descending stroke is its handle. That makes the letter and tool the same shape, rather than decorating a letter with another object.

Recommended execution: keep the y's familiar branching silhouette; gently flatten the inner faces of its arms into opposing jaw surfaces and shape the descending stroke as a restrained handle. No moving jaw, teeth, hole, outlines, metal texture, or extra protrusions. It should read **hoys first, tool second**. Prototype this rather than assume it will work: if the jaw geometry makes the y look like a u, v, or standalone symbol, reject it.

An open-ended wrench is a stronger broad repair-and-maintenance cue than a shovel, although no tool represents every trade. A shovel-shaped y tail could be elegant, but its blade points more strongly toward landscaping or excavation. Keep it as a deliberate challenger, not the lead recommendation.

1. **Quiet authority / wrench-y — recommended.** Refined humanist sans-serif lettering, moderate weight, open counters, and controlled curves. Integrate the wrench into the y's upper fork and descending handle. Strongest balance of recognition, simplicity, and AI-company scale.
2. **Precision / wrench-y.** A materially different architectural sans treatment: straighter strokes, carefully chamfered jaw faces, and a more upright, engineered y. Compare against the first concept to see whether precision or warmth better suits Hoys; not a recolor of the same lettering.
3. **Editorial craft / shovel-y.** A contemporary, robust low-contrast serif design. Let the y's descending stroke terminate in a small, softly squared spade blade that remains continuous with the letter. No detached shovel, shaft ornament, or oversized blade. Test whether it reads as purposeful craft rather than a gardening company.
4. **Fitted intelligence / coupling-h.** A bespoke restrained grotesque where the h's shoulder and stem meet through one subtle fitting-like collar. The h stays intact, with no gap that breaks the letter. A quieter mechanical reference; reject if it merely looks like a font defect or communicates plumbing too narrowly.

### Motifs not to pursue
- A roof over the h: quickly reads as a generic real-estate/home-contractor logo.
- An o full of screw slots, bolts, or gear teeth: either too detailed at small sizes or too close to a settings icon.
- Tools dangling beside or underneath the letters: separate decoration rather than a natural fit.
- Multiple tools across the name: becomes a rebus, not an elegant company identity.

## Color exploration
Treat these as proposed comparison palettes, not a decision to recolor the website.
- **Ink + warm white:** near-black `#202321`, warm white `#FAF8F3`. Strongest starting point for timelessness and broad use.
- **Cobalt + warm white:** cobalt `#1E3FBF`, warm white `#FAF8F3`. Connects to the current site, with a single-color logo rather than multicolored letters.
- **Deep green + white:** mineral green `#244A40`, white `#FFFFFF`. A quieter alternative expressing grounded confidence; compare against cobalt rather than assume it is better.
- Avoid gradients, chrome, glow, primary lime lettering, AI sparkles, and tool-colored decorations. Every concept must work in one color first.

## Deliverables after approval
- Four initial logo concepts as PNGs, shown at equal optical size on a clean comparison sheet.
- For each concept: a transparent PNG plus a light-background preview; inspect dark placements and provide a reversed version where required.
- Recommend one direction based on distinctiveness, immediate recognition of **hoys**, a discoverable home-services cue, fit with the current site, and flexibility beyond home services.
- Refine the chosen candidate from its actual image, then deliver tightly framed transparent light/dark PNGs with useful margins.
- No website replacement or site-wide rename until you choose and authorize the logo.

## Production and checks
Use the logo-design workflow and premium image generation for bespoke lettering. Render the exact name **hoys** with no extra words. Inspect every result at full size and at approximately 80–120 px wide / 24–32 px high; compare a 30 px-high placement against the current header. Test each in a flat single-color silhouette: the integrated tool must not break the letter, increase its apparent size disproportionately, or create awkward spacing with its neighbors. Reject malformed letters, pale removal fringes, inconsistent spacing, distracting tools, and fragile small details. PNG is the requested deliverable, not an editable vector; vector redraw can be a separate later step.
